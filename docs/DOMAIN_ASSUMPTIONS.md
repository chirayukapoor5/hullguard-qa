# Domain assumptions

- X is longitudinal, Y transverse and Z vertical. Positions are minimum corners in a right-handed frame, with dimensions ordered X, Y, Z. Z = 0 is the upper face of the bottom plate. The bottom grows downward for the thickness scenario, keeping the other components' coordinates fixed.
- Geometry is in mm. The accepted coordinate precision is 0.001 mm. Dimensions must be at least 0.001 mm, and coordinates and dimensions are bounded to 1,000,000 mm. The project accepts up to 200 components.
- The material table contains one example steel with density 7,850 kg/m³. This density is a fixture assumption. The mass output unit is kg. One cubic millimetre equals 10⁻⁹ m³.
- Each part is an axis-aligned rectangular solid. The thickness axis is Z for the bottom, Y for side plates and longitudinal flat bars, and X for transverse bulkheads. One bottom is required. Every other part sits on its upper face and fits within its XY footprint. Side plates are on the transverse edges. Longitudinal stiffeners extend farther in X than in Y or Z.
- Face, edge and point contact are allowed. Positive-volume intersections are rejected. In the baseline, split stiffeners meet the bulkhead and the bulkhead fits between the side plates. Assembly mass sums disjoint configured volumes. Missing optional parts are detected through artifact checks against a supplied source or as removals in a revision comparison.
- The SVG is a schematic XY plan with a drawing schedule. For the top-down rendering, Z is represented by annotations and metadata. The OBJ subset uses named objects and triangular faces. Coordinates stay in mm, with units declared in a HULLGUARD comment.
- Exported geometry and metadata are reconciled to 0.000001 mm. Mass reconciliation accepts 0.000001 kg absolute error or 10⁻¹² relative error, whichever is larger. The manifest and drawing store source precision. The BOM retains sufficient digits to meet its tolerances.
- The fixture represents an open structural bay. Curved or rotated geometry, profiles with voids, brackets, welds, structural strength, class approval, production drawings, watertightness, center of gravity, FEM and native CAD interoperability are outside scope.
